Revising Drawings Without Renumbering Everything

Part marks, drawing numbers and revision marks are three different things. Move the one you meant to move, and leave the marks the shop is already cutting to.

A Question

“We have a revision that affects only some parts. How do we revise those drawings without Tekla Structures renumbering all the parts in the model?”

B Short Answer
Short answer

Two different numbers are involved, and keeping them apart is most of the answer.

Part marks come from numbering. Drawing revisions come from Document manager.

  1. Open Numbering settings and leave Renumber all clear.
  2. Select Compare to old, or Keep number if possible.
  3. Click Perform numbering, then Number modified objects.
  4. Check the Numbering preview, then click Assign numbers.
  5. In Document manager, select the drawings and click Revision.
  6. Enter the mark and description, then click Create.

Renumber all wipes existing numbers, and the documentation warns that existing drawings are removed too.

Issue Summary

A revision arrives, a few parts change, and someone runs numbering. Suddenly marks that were issued to the shop three weeks ago have moved. The fabricator is cutting to one set of numbers and the model now says something else.

The confusion behind it is that Tekla Structures carries several numbers that people all call “the number”. The part mark, the drawing number and the drawing revision mark are three separate things, produced by three separate mechanisms.

Getting a clean revision out means knowing which one you actually want to move, and then keeping your hands off the other two.

Three Numbers, Three Mechanisms

Number Where it comes from What moves it
Part and assembly position numberThe numbering series on the part, resolved by running numbering.Running numbering. What changes and what stays depends entirely on the Numbering Setup dialog.
Drawing name and numberThe drawing itself, listed in Document manager.Editing it in Document manager, or the drawing properties that created it.
Revision markA revision record created against a drawing in Document manager.You, deliberately, through the Revision handling dialog. Nothing creates it automatically.

So “revise the drawing number without renumbering the parts” is not one setting. It is two habits: number conservatively, and revise explicitly.

Keeping Existing Part Marks Stable

This is the half that goes wrong. The Numbering Setup dialog decides whether a modified part keeps its identity or takes a fresh mark.

LEAVE IT OFF

Renumber all

All parts get a new number and all information on previous numbers is lost. The documentation also states that any existing drawings will be removed. On a job with issued drawings this is not a recovery step, it is a restart.

THE SAFE ONE

Compare to old

The part gets the same number as a previously numbered similar part. A modified part that still matches something already numbered falls back into that existing mark rather than inventing a new one.

GENTLEST

Keep number if possible

Modified parts maintain their previous numbers if possible. The right choice when material is already in fabrication and stability matters more than tidiness.

DELIBERATE

Take new number

The part gets a new number even if a similar numbered part already exists. Use it when a change must be visible as a new mark on the shop floor, and know that you are choosing it.

CAREFUL

Re-use old numbers

Tekla Structures reuses the numbers of parts that have been deleted. Tidy on a job with lots of deletions, and dangerous once those old numbers are already on paper somewhere.

DO NOT TOUCH

The property checkboxes

Holes, part name, orientation, welds, surface treatment and the tolerance all decide what counts as identical. Changing any of them mid-project reshuffles marks across the whole model, and it looks exactly like a bug.

The Procedure

  1. 1

    Confirm the numbering settings before you touch anything

    On the Drawings & reports tab, click Numbering settings > Numbering settings. Confirm Renumber all is clear, and that the comparison option is the one your job has been using all along.

    Do not improve the settings during a revision

    A revision is the worst moment to tidy up the numbering rules. Adjust the settings early in the project, before creating drawings or reports, and not in the middle of a project. If the settings are wrong, that is a separate conversation with the whole team.

  2. 2

    Number only what changed

    On the Drawings & reports tab, click Perform numbering > Number modified objects. This numbers the parts, assemblies, cast units and reinforcement created or modified since the last numbering, and leaves everything else alone.

  3. 3

    Read the preview before you accept it

    The Numbering preview dialog shows the proposed position numbers before they are assigned. This is your last chance to notice that four hundred parts are about to change mark.

    When the proposal looks right, click Assign numbers. If it does not, stop and find out why rather than accepting and fixing later.

  4. 4

    Let the drawings catch up

    Drawings update from the model once numbering is settled. Check the changes and status columns in Document manager to see which drawings are affected by the revision.

  5. 5

    Create the revision record

    In Document manager, select the drawings to revise and click Revision. The Revision handling dialog opens.

    Enter the Mark, using numbers such as 1, 2, 3 or letters such as A, B, C, then fill in Created by, Date, Description and any of Checked by, Approved by, Delivery, Info 1 and Info 2 that your office uses. Click Create.

  6. 6

    Put the mark on the sheet

    The revision mark now shows in Document manager and appears in the revision table if the drawing has one. To mark up the changed area on the drawing itself, open the drawing and use Revision mark on the Annotations tab.

    The revision must exist in Document manager first. The revision mark draws its information from that record, so there is nothing to place until it is created.

  7. 7

    Export and issue

    Export the affected drawings to DWG from Document manager, and send only those. A revision issue that quietly includes untouched sheets invites the fabricator to work from the wrong one.

Editing a Revision You Already Created

Revisions are records, not one-way switches. Select the drawing in Document manager, click Revision, choose the revision from the Rev.No. list, change what needs changing, and click Modify.

That is the right way to correct a typo in a revision description. Deleting and recreating a revision to fix a spelling mistake loses the history that the revision existed at all.

When Marks Moved Anyway

If parts you never touched have new marks, work through these in order.

Was Renumber all selected? Check the Numbering Setup dialog. This is the most common cause and the most damaging one.

Did the numbering settings change? Someone switching the holes or orientation checkbox will reshuffle marks across the entire model on the next numbering run, without touching a single part.

Was a numbering series edited? Changing a prefix or start number on parts that already carry marks moves them at the next run, by design.

Did a series overflow into another? If beams start at 1 and columns at 100, a job with 120 beam marks collides. Tekla Structures warns about overlapping position numbers, and objects with overlapping numbers will not be numbered at all.
