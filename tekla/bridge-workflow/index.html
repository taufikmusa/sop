B Short Answer
Short answer

A bridge model starts from the alignment, not from a grid.

Get the road or rail geometry in first. Everything else is positioned against it.

  1. Obtain the alignment, normally as a LandXML file.
  2. Bring it in, or use a Tekla construction line.
  3. Define the stations where the bridge starts and ends.
  4. Define the key cross sections at those stations.
  5. Let Bridge Creator interpolate the deck between them.
  6. Detail, reinforce, number and draw as normal.

In 2026 Bridge Creator is a standard plug-in that stores its data inside the model.

Issue Summary

Bridge work breaks the habit every building modeller has. A building is laid out on a rectangular grid. A bridge is laid out along an alignment, a curve in plan and profile that the road or rail engineer owns, and everything structural is positioned relative to that curve rather than to a grid intersection.

That single difference drives the whole workflow. Get the alignment in correctly and the rest is ordinary Tekla Structures detailing. Get it wrong, or start modelling before it exists, and every element is in the wrong place in a way that is painful to correct.

The tool that bridges the gap, so to speak, is Bridge Creator. It reads road and rail alignments in LandXML format and lets you define key cross sections at specific chainages, which it then interpolates to generate the deck, including complex double curved concrete decks.

Before You Start

ALIGNMENT

You need the geometry from the civil side

Bridge Creator takes alignment data as LandXML files, or any Tekla construction line. Without one of those you have nothing to position against, so this is the first thing to ask for, not the last.

VERSION

Bridge Creator changed in 2026

It is documented as an extension for Tekla Structures 2018 and later, available in Tekla Warehouse. In 2026 it was re-engineered as a standard plug-in that stores its configuration data inside the model component objects, removing the dependency on external files.

INSTALL

How you get it depends on your version

For the extension route, download the package from Tekla Warehouse, double-click the .tsep installer, and select which Tekla Structures versions to install to. The package also contains example models worth opening before your own job.

MATERIAL

Bridges are rarely one material

A deck may be cast in place, the beams precast, the parapets steel. Each of those has its own workflow in this vault, and a bridge job normally runs two or three of them at once.

STATIONS

Chainages are the language

Positions along a bridge are given as stations or chainages, not as X and Y. Agree with the civil engineer which datum and which units before you start placing anything.

SKEW

Expect skew and superelevation

The tool handles varying superelevation, skew angles and multiple reference lines. Knowing which of these your bridge has changes how you define the key sections.

The Workflow, Stage by Stage

  1. 1

    Get the alignment

    Obtain the road or rail alignment from the civil engineer, normally as a LandXML file. If no LandXML exists, any Tekla construction line can serve as the reference geometry instead.

  2. 2

    Set up Bridge Creator

    On Tekla Structures 2026 it is a standard plug-in. On earlier versions, download it from Tekla Warehouse and install the .tsep package, selecting the versions to install to.

    Check where your configuration data lives

    Before 2026, Bridge Creator configuration sat in external files. From 2026 it is stored inside the model component objects. If you are moving a job between versions, or picking up someone else model, know which behaviour you are dealing with before you go looking for missing settings.

  3. 3

    Define the stations

    Set the start and end chainages for the bridge object. Tekla Structures 2026 adds a centralised station library, with rule-based logic that handles station creation and definition with less effort.

  4. 4

    Define the key cross sections

    Define the cross-sectional profiles at specific stations. These are the shapes the deck must pass through, and they are where the real design decisions are captured.

  5. 5

    Let the deck interpolate

    Bridge Creator interpolates between the key sections to generate the deck, including double curved concrete decks and varying superelevation. Check the result against the key sections before building anything on top of it.

  6. 6

    Place the substructure and details

    Model the abutments, piers, bearings, parapets and drainage. Placement of components or beams on curved alignments uses chord-based positioning in 2026, which makes placing straight objects along a curved alignment more consistent.

  7. 7

    Reinforce

    Deck, pier and abutment reinforcement follows the ordinary concrete reinforcement workflow: reinforcement components where they fit, rebar sets where they do not.

  8. 8

    Number, draw and report

    From here the job behaves like any other. Set the numbering series, number the model, create the drawings, and run the reports.

  9. 9

    Hand over

    Export IFC for coordination with the civil and geotechnical models, and produce the production data your fabricators need: NC files for steel, BVBS for reinforcement, precast exports for precast elements.

What Makes Bridges Harder Than Buildings

GEOMETRY

Nothing is orthogonal

Curved in plan, graded in profile, cross-falls that change along the length. A detail that works at one chainage may not fit fifty metres along, which is why key sections are defined at several stations rather than once.

OWNERSHIP

The alignment is not yours

It comes from the civil design and it changes. Build the model so that a revised alignment is a re-run rather than a remodel, and keep the LandXML you were issued alongside the job.

MIXED TRADES

Several workflows in one model

Cast in situ deck, precast beams, steel parapets and heavy reinforcement can all appear in one bridge. Each needs its own settings, its own numbering series and its own deliverable.

SIZE

Models get heavy

The re-engineered Bridge Creator in 2026 reduces processing time in complex bridge modelling. On older versions, expect long regeneration times and plan your view and work area discipline accordingly.

STAGING

Construction sequence matters

Bridges are built in stages, and pour sequencing for the cast-in-place parts is part of the design rather than an afterthought. Pour management covers that side.

SURVEY

Setting out data goes back to site

Positions are only useful to site in the same chainage language the civil team uses. Confirm the datum before issuing anything for setting out.

Being Honest About the Documentation

Tekla User Assistance documents Bridge Creator itself, and the 2026 release notes document what changed. What it does not publish is a single step-by-step bridge workflow article in the product guides, in the way that concrete detailing or analysis has one.

The most detailed published material is the Bridge Creator User Guide issued for 2026, and a bridge design workflow course covering the route from road data through fabrication and erection. Those are the right places to go for the detail beyond this page.

Plan the first bridge with help

A first bridge in Tekla Structures is not the job to learn alignments on alone. The example models shipped with Bridge Creator are worth opening first, and your local Tekla Support can point you at the training that fits your bridge type.
