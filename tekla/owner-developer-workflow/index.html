The Owner and Developer Workflow

You are probably not going to model anything. What decides whether the model is worth anything to you is what you require from the people who do, written down before they start.

A Question

“What is the workflow to use Tekla Structures as an owner or developer? We commission the buildings rather than detail them, and we want to know what the model is worth to us.”

Also asked as

  • Do we need Tekla Structures as a client, or just the output from it?
  • What should we ask our consultants and fabricators to deliver?
  • How do we see progress and status without buying a modelling seat?
  • Can we get quantities out of the model for cost checking?
  • How do we receive and view the model our supply chain builds?

Generic form of a real customer question — names, company and project details removed. Tekla Structures 2026.

B Short Answer
Short answer

Most owners do not need a Tekla Structures seat. They need what comes out of one.

Your job is specifying the deliverable and having somewhere to receive it.

  1. Name the IFC schema and the property sets you require.
  2. Ask for the model in a Trimble Connect project you can reach.
  3. Agree who owns the building hierarchy and the phases.
  4. Require quantities as reports, not a spreadsheet someone retyped.
  5. Ask for status through Project Status Visualization or an equivalent.
  6. Put all of that in the appointment, before work starts.

Tools such as Task Manager are documented for contractors and project managers, not owners.

Issue Summary

This page is different from the others in this set, and the difference is worth saying out loud before anything else: an owner or developer is usually not a Tekla Structures user.

Every other workflow here ends at something being made. A fabricator produces NC files, a precaster produces Unitechnik, a rebar shop produces BVBS. You do not make anything. You commission it, pay for it, and eventually own and operate it. That means the useful question is not “how do we use Tekla Structures” but “what should we require from the people who do, and where do we receive it”.

The good news is that the answer is concrete rather than vague. Everything an owner reasonably wants from a construction model already exists as a specific output of Tekla Structures. The failure mode is not technical, it is contractual: nobody asked for it in writing at the start, so it was never produced.

Buying a seat is rarely the answer

A Tekla Structures licence is a detailing tool. Buying one so that somebody in your office can open models is usually the expensive way to solve a problem that a shared Trimble Connect project and a well-written appointment solve better.

What You Are Actually Asking For

GEOMETRY

An IFC you can open

IFC export supports the IFC 2X3, IFC 4 and IFC 4.3 schemas. Name the one you want. A model delivered in a schema your own tools cannot read is not a deliverable, it is a file.

DATA

Property sets, named

Model objects are exported on the basis of the export settings, including the property sets. The properties you care about come across only if somebody chose to include them. Ask for them by name.

ACCESS

A place to receive it

Trimble Connect is a collaboration tool that connects the constructible data and enables architects, engineers, contractors, owners and operators to collaborate in building projects. Owners are named in that list on purpose.

QUANTITIES

Numbers straight from the model

Reports are created directly from the model database, so the information is always accurate. A quantity that came out of the model beats one that was retyped into a spreadsheet on the way to you.

STRUCTURE

An agreed breakdown

The building hierarchy runs project, site, building, building section, building storey, space. Agree it before modelling starts and every report you ever ask for lands in a shape you recognise.

STATUS

Progress you can see

Project Status Visualization reviews the status of model objects in a specific time frame, for example displaying the erection schedule for groups of parts in different colours.

The Workflow, Stage by Stage

  1. 1

    Write the requirements before the appointment is signed

    This is the whole job, and it happens before anyone models anything. Name the IFC schema, the property sets, the reporting breakdown, the reporting frequency and the platform you will receive everything on.

    Asking for these at practical completion gets you an apology. Asking for them in the appointment gets you the data.

  2. 2

    Set up the place everything lands

    Create the Trimble Connect project and make sure your own people can reach it. A Tekla Structures model can be uploaded directly as a .tekla file, and model objects can be exported as .ifc reference models into a Trimble Connect project.

  3. 3

    Agree the breakdown structure

    Decide with the design and construction team who owns the building hierarchy and the phases, and what they mean. Phases are often used to indicate erection sequences, and they are the natural hook between the model and your programme.

    Whoever sets the structure sets your reporting forever

    If the contractor organises the model around their own trade packages and nobody asks otherwise, every quantity you receive for the next two years arrives in their shape rather than yours. This is a five-minute conversation at the start and a very expensive one later.

  4. 4

    Receive and view the model

    Open what you were sent, in Trimble Connect or in whatever IFC viewer your organisation uses. Check that the property sets you asked for are actually present, on the first issue rather than the tenth.

  5. 5

    Read the quantities

    Ask for reports generated from the model, and for the Organizer breakdown behind them. Organizer categorises the model by location and by property, and the Object Browser exports property values straight to Excel.

    What you are buying here is traceability: a number that can be pointed back at the objects it came from, rather than a total you have to take on trust.

  6. 6

    Track progress

    Project Status Visualization identifies the parts scheduled to be fabricated during a specific time period, and can display the erection schedule for groups of parts in different colours. That is a far better monthly report than a photograph of a site.

    Where the supply chain runs a schedule in Microsoft Office Project or Primavera P6, Task Manager can import it and link the tasks to model objects, which turns the programme into something you can look at rather than read.

  7. 7

    Keep what you will need later

    The model is also an asset record. Decide at the start what version you want handed over at completion, in which schema, with which properties, and where it will live afterwards. Deciding that at handover is too late, because by then the team that could produce it has demobilised.

Being Honest About the Documentation

Tekla User Assistance documents every tool named above in detail. What it does not publish is an owner or developer workflow, and that absence is informative rather than accidental.

Task Manager is described as a tool for contractors, sub-contractors and project managers. Quantity takeoff guidance is published as contractor material. The product is written for the people who produce the model, not for the people who commission it. Where owners do appear in the documentation is in the description of Trimble Connect, as one of the parties collaborating on a project rather than as operators of the modelling software.

So treat this page as what it is: a translation of a detailing product into the questions an owner actually has. The underlying facts are all documented. The framing is not, and anyone who tells you there is an official owner workflow in Tekla Structures is describing something that does not exist.

Judge a proposal by the deliverables, not the software

A supply chain that promises a BIM model and cannot name the schema, the property sets or the reporting breakdown has not thought about your side of it. Those three answers tell you more about how the job will run than any amount of talk about which software is being used.

If You Do End Up With a Seat

Some developers, particularly those with an in-house construction arm or a repeated building type, genuinely do model. If that is you, the page you want is the contractor workflow rather than this one: building hierarchy, phases, Organizer, quantity takeoff and Trimble Connect are the same tools, used directly instead of specified.

The other honest route, where the volume justifies it, is to require the supply chain to model to your standard rather than their own. Your firm folder, your numbering scheme, your report templates, your property sets. That is a real strategy, it costs something to set up, and it pays back across a portfolio rather than on a single job.
